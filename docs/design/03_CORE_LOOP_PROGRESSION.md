# 03 — Core Loop & Progression

> **2.0 revision:** Discovery rewards initiate the loop; hauling, light production and shared capability upgrades create further exploration and replanning goals. Safe starting space teaches rewards before hazards. Details and save contracts are in `docs/design/DIG_DOWN_2_0_DESIGN.md`; V1 pacing below is historical, not a verified 2.0 playtime target.

**Status: LOCKED structure / PROVISIONAL pacing**

## 1. Design thesis
Progression is not primarily “more buildings and larger numbers.” It is the player's growing ability to read increasingly complex underground conditions and reshape the burrow around them.

## 2. Minute-to-minute loop
### Observe
- current food/rest/storage pressure;
- work queue and mouse traffic;
- frontier signals;
- room conditions;
- water/stability warnings.

### Predict
The player forms a qualitative hypothesis: food likely near dense roots, water likely below wet soil, a cavity may exist behind crumbling earth.

### Commit
Designate a dig line/area. Commitment has time and topology costs even before a hazard occurs.

### Reveal
Excavation exposes new cells/features.

### React
The environment or colony responds automatically.

### Adapt
Player changes routing/zones, digs relief space, seals, reinforces, or changes priorities.

### Settle
Mice resume normal life and turn the new configuration into home.

## 3. 5-minute cadence target
Every 3–7 minutes of active play, the game should ideally deliver at least one of:
- a meaningful new signal;
- a new useful space;
- a discovery reaction;
- a spatial reorganization;
- a clear chapter-progress reveal.

Avoid long stretches of mechanically identical empty digging.

## 4. 30–45 minute Vertical Slice arc
### 0–5 min
- 3 mice, cramped starting burrow.
- tutorial teaches dig designation and camera.
- first room expansion.

### 5–12 min
- food pressure appears.
- root signal is introduced.
- player follows or ignores it.
- Root Cluster becomes first opportunity/obstacle.

### 12–20 min
- zone system: Sleeping and Storage.
- player experiences path and room-shape consequences.

### 20–30 min
- moisture signal appears.
- player may prepare drainage or approach directly.
- Water Reservoir breach demonstrates systemic reaction.

### 30–40 min
- unstable soil appears near a valuable route.
- player learns warning/reinforcement or chooses a detour.

### 40–45 min
- stabilize colony and reach slice objective.
- show burrow history/result, not a score screen.

## 5. Player progression layers
### 5.1 Knowledge progression — primary
Players personally learn signal meanings and physical rules.

### 5.2 Colony interpretation progression — secondary
Repeated encounters can improve UI clarity without revealing exact hidden data.
Example: unknown dampness → recognized damp soil → stronger “water likely nearby” interpretation.

### 5.3 Capability progression — restrained
New contextual tools should open new solutions, not obsolete old problems.
Examples:
- simple seal;
- support/reinforcement;
- priority dig;
- optional drainage designation.

### 5.4 Population progression
More mice increase throughput but also food/rest/traffic pressure. Population growth must create spatial consequences.

## 6. Chapter progression
### Chapter 1: Settle
New concepts:
- free excavation;
- basic terrain hardness;
- roots;
- food;
- zones;
- simple pathing.

Mastery test:
Player maintains food/rest while creating a functional small burrow.

### Chapter 2: Adapt
New concepts:
- water;
- unstable soil;
- foundation materials;
- stronger travel/topology constraints;
- heat/sound cues where required.

Mastery test:
Player deliberately engineers around environmental consequences rather than simply repairing them.

### Chapter 3: Old Burrow
New concepts:
- pre-existing cavities;
- blocked legacy routes;
- traces/landmarks;
- final warm stable zone;
- reuse versus new excavation.

Mastery test:
Player integrates old and new spaces into a stable final colony.

## 7. Seasonal meta-pressure
**PROVISIONAL:** A broad “winter approaching” timeline provides direction without minute-to-minute stress.

Rules:
- It should not force speedrunning.
- It should make prolonged disorder meaningful.
- Milestones may slow/advance presentation, but the timer must remain forgiving.
- Avoid daily micromanagement calendars in V1.

## 8. Reward model
Rewards should favor:
- new information;
- safer/faster spatial options;
- access to special terrain;
- visual colony enrichment;
- chapter progress.

Avoid excessive currency rewards disconnected from space.

## 9. Anti-strip-mining controls
The game must not rely on invisible bans. Use systemic costs:
- mouse travel time;
- dig time by material;
- room fragmentation;
- hazard exposure;
- unstable spans;
- unnecessary frontier area;
- seasonal opportunity cost.

If players can excavate the whole map with no downside, the loop has failed.

## 10. Anti-single-optimal-layout controls
Worlds should vary in:
- root locations;
- wet pockets;
- hard rock masses;
- cavity templates;
- start geometry;
- route bottlenecks;
- landmark positions.

Room suitability and travel costs should make adaptation preferable to reproducing one rectangle every run.

## 11. Endgame philosophy
The final home should look like a synthesis of encountered constraints rather than a cleanly planned city. Completion should celebrate the burrow map and its history.
